# What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the **protection of cyberspace, digital systems, networks, applications, and information from cyber threats and cyberspace vulnerabilities**.

* It protects information and systems from attacks performed through cyberspace.
* It deals mainly with **deliberate/malicious acts** such as hacking, malware, phishing, ransomware, cyber espionage, etc.
* It focuses on threats **via cyberspace**, not necessarily threats *to* cyberspace itself.
* It generally does **not deal with physical security** like theft of hardware, fire, flood, or physical break-ins (handled by physical security controls).

## Simple Definition

> Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting systems, networks, applications, and data from unauthorized access, attack, damage, or disruption through cyberspace.

---

# Cyberspace

Cyberspace is the virtual environment formed by:

* Internet
* Networks
* Computers
* Cloud systems
* Mobile devices
* Applications
* Digital communication systems

Example:

* Email systems
* Banking apps
* Social media
* Government portals
* Cloud servers

---

# Objectives of Cybersecurity

## 1. Confidentiality

Ensure information is accessible only to authorized users.

Example:

* Password protection
* Encryption

---

## 2. Integrity

Ensure data is accurate and not modified without authorization.

Example:

* Hashing
* Digital signatures

---

## 3. Availability

Ensure systems and data are available when needed.

Example:

* Backup systems
* Redundancy
* DDoS protection

# Non-Repudiation

Non-repudiation means:

> A person cannot deny performing an action.

Used in:

* Digital signatures
* Online banking
* Secure transactions

Example:

* Sender cannot deny sending an email signed with a digital signature.

---

# Authenticity

Authenticity ensures:

> The user, message, or system is genuine and verified.

Methods:

* Passwords
* OTP
* Biometrics
* Certificates

Example:

* Verifying a website using HTTPS certificates.

---

# What is Hacking?

There is **no universally accepted standard definition** of hacking.

Generally:

> Hacking is the process of finding, exploiting, or manipulating weaknesses in systems, networks, applications, or devices.

Important Notes:

* Media often spreads misleading or exaggerated information about hackers.
* Technically, hacking itself is a skill/activity.
* Intent determines legality and ethics.
* Terms like “good hacker” and “bad hacker” are informal/non-technical classifications.

# Important Terminologies

---

## Vulnerability

A weakness in a system that can be exploited.

Examples:

* Weak passwords
* Unpatched software
* Misconfiguration

---

## Threat

Anything capable of causing harm to a system or information.

Examples:

* Malware
* Hackers
* Insider attacks
* Phishing

---

## Risk

The possibility of loss when a threat exploits a vulnerability.

### Formula

\text{Risk} = \text{Threat} \times \text{Vulnerability} \times \text{Impact}

Example:

* Weak password + brute force attack = account compromise risk

---

## Asset

Anything valuable to an organization.

Examples:

* Data
* Servers
* Applications
* Customer records
* Intellectual property

---

## Bug

An error/flaw in software or hardware.

Examples:

* Coding mistake
* Logic flaw
* Memory corruption

Not all bugs are vulnerabilities, but some can become vulnerabilities.

---

## Exploit

Code, technique, or method used to take advantage of a vulnerability.

Example:

* SQL Injection payload
* Buffer overflow exploit

---

## Zero-Day Exploit

An exploit targeting a vulnerability unknown to the vendor or without an available patch.

“Zero-day” means:

* Developer had **zero days** to fix it before exploitation.

---

# Information Security (InfoSec)

Information Security is the protection of:

* Information
* Information systems
* Data confidentiality, integrity, and availability

InfoSec is broader than cybersecurity.

## Difference

| InfoSec                              | Cybersecurity                          |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| Protects information in all forms    | Protects digital systems in cyberspace |
| Includes physical + digital security | Mainly focuses on digital threats      |
| Broader domain                       | Subdomain of InfoSec                   |

---

# Lock Picking (Security Analogy)

Lock picking is often used as an analogy in cybersecurity.

| Physical Security | Cybersecurity      |
| ----------------- | ------------------ |
| Lock              | Authentication     |
| Lock picking      | Bypassing security |
| Physical key      | Password/token     |

Purpose:

* Demonstrates weaknesses in protection mechanisms.

---

# VA, PT, and VAPT

## Vulnerability Assessment (VA)

Process of identifying and listing vulnerabilities.

* Finds weaknesses
* Usually does not exploit them

---

## Penetration Testing (PT)

Simulated attack to exploit vulnerabilities.

* Tests real-world impact
* Demonstrates exploitability

---

## VAPT

Combination of:

* Vulnerability Assessment
* Penetration Testing

Purpose:

* Identify + validate vulnerabilities

# Cyber Espionage

Cyber espionage is:

> Unauthorized digital spying to steal sensitive information.

Targets:

* Governments
* Military
* Corporations
* Research organizations

Examples:

* Stealing confidential documents
* State-sponsored attacks

---

# Security Assessment

Overall evaluation of an organization's security posture.

Includes:

* Risk assessment
* Vulnerability assessment
* Penetration testing
* Compliance review

---

# Security Audit

A formal examination of security controls, policies, and compliance.

Focus:

* Standards
* Policies
* Regulatory requirements

Examples:

* ISO 27001 audit
* PCI-DSS audit

---

# Security Audit vs VAPT

| Security Audit                 | VAPT                            |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| Checks compliance and policies | Finds technical vulnerabilities |
| Documentation-focused          | Attack-focused                  |
| Verifies controls              | Exploits weaknesses             |
| Usually non-invasive           | Can be invasive                 |

---

# Important One-Liners (Exam/Interview)

* Cybersecurity protects cyberspace against cyber threats.
* CIA triad = Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability.
* Vulnerability = weakness.
* Threat = potential danger.
* Risk = possibility of loss.
* Exploit = method/code to abuse vulnerability.
* Zero-day = unknown vulnerability without patch.
* VAPT = Vulnerability Assessment + Penetration Testing.
* InfoSec is broader than cybersecurity.
* Non-repudiation prevents denial of actions.
* Authenticity verifies genuineness.

# Common Interview Questions

## Q1. Difference between threat and vulnerability?

* Threat = potential attacker/danger
* Vulnerability = weakness exploitable by threat

---

## Q2. Difference between VA and PT?

* VA identifies vulnerabilities.
* PT actively exploits vulnerabilities.

---

## Q3. What is the CIA triad?

* Confidentiality
* Integrity
* Availability

---

## Q4. What is a zero-day attack?

Attack exploiting an unknown/unpatched vulnerability.

---

## Q5. Is cybersecurity same as InfoSec?

No.

* Cybersecurity focuses on digital/cyber threats.
* InfoSec protects information in all forms.
