# 1. Cyber Crimes

## Definition

A cyber crime is any unlawful act where a computer, network, digital device, or internet is used as a tool, target, or both.

### Cyber Crime Categories

| Type                  | Description                              |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Against Individuals   | Identity theft, cyber stalking, phishing |
| Against Property      | Data theft, malware attacks              |
| Against Organizations | Ransomware, DDoS attacks                 |
| Against Society       | Fake news, hate speech                   |
| Against Government    | Cyber warfare, espionage                 |

---

## Characteristics of Cyber Crimes

* Global reach
* Anonymity of attackers
* High speed execution
* Low cost
* Difficult attribution
* Borderless nature

---

## Common Cyber Crimes

### 1. Hacking

Unauthorized access to a system.

### 2. Phishing

Fake emails/websites used to steal credentials.

### 3. Identity Theft

Stealing personal information.

### 4. Cyber Stalking

Online harassment or monitoring.

### 5. Ransomware

Encrypts files and demands payment.

### 6. Malware Attacks

Viruses, worms, Trojans, spyware.

### 7. Data Breach

Unauthorized exposure of sensitive data.

### 8. Denial of Service (DoS/DDoS)

Making services unavailable.

### 9. Online Banking Fraud

Stealing financial information.

### 10. Social Engineering

Manipulating people to reveal secrets.

# 7. Introduction to Ethical Hacking

## Definition

Ethical hacking is the authorized process of testing systems to identify vulnerabilities before attackers exploit them.

Ethical hackers:

* Work legally
* Obtain permission
* Report vulnerabilities
* Help improve security

---

## Goals

* Identify vulnerabilities
* Assess security posture
* Reduce risk
* Improve defenses

---

# Ethical Hacker vs Malicious Hacker

| Ethical Hacker    | Malicious Hacker  |
| ----------------- | ----------------- |
| Authorized        | Unauthorized      |
| Legal             | Illegal           |
| Reports findings  | Exploits findings |
| Improves security | Causes damage     |

---

# 8. Ethical Hacking Terminology

## Vulnerability

Weakness in a system.

Example:
Outdated software.

---

## Threat

Potential danger.

Example:
Attacker.

---

## Risk

Likelihood of threat exploiting vulnerability.

Risk = Threat × Vulnerability

---

## Exploit

Code that abuses a vulnerability.

---

## Payload

Action performed after exploitation.

Example:
Reverse shell.

---

## Backdoor

Hidden access mechanism.

---

## Zero-Day

Unknown vulnerability with no patch.

---

## Patch

Security update fixing vulnerabilities.

---

## Footprinting

Information gathering.

---

## Reconnaissance

Initial intelligence collection.

---

## Enumeration

Extracting detailed information.

---

## Privilege Escalation

Gaining higher permissions.

---

## Persistence

Maintaining access.

# 10. Phases of Ethical Hacking

## Phase 1: Reconnaissance (Footprinting)

Collect information about target.

Passive:

* Search engines
* WHOIS
* Social media

Active:

* DNS queries
* Network scans

---

## Phase 2: Scanning

Identify live systems and services.

Activities:

* Port scanning
* Service detection
* Vulnerability scanning

---

## Phase 3: Enumeration

Gather detailed information.

Examples:

* User accounts
* Shares
* Services

---

## Phase 4: Gaining Access

Testing vulnerabilities.

Examples:

* SQL Injection
* Password attacks

---

## Phase 5: Maintaining Access

Testing persistence possibilities.

Examples:

* Backdoors
* Scheduled tasks

---

## Phase 6: Covering Tracks

Attackers delete evidence.

Ethical hackers generally document instead of hiding activities.

---

## Ethical Hacking Methodology Flow

Reconnaissance
↓
Scanning
↓
Enumeration
↓
Gaining Access
↓
Maintaining Access
↓
Reporting
