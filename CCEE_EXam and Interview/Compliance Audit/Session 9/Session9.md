# Session 10: HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act) Notes

---

# 1. Introduction to HIPAA

**HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)** is a U.S. federal law enacted in **1996** to protect sensitive healthcare information and improve the efficiency of the healthcare system.

### Main Objectives

* Protect patient health information.
* Improve healthcare data security.
* Standardize electronic healthcare transactions.
* Prevent healthcare fraud and abuse.
* Ensure health insurance portability.

---

# Why HIPAA is Important?

Before HIPAA:

* Patient records were easily exposed.
* No standardized protection mechanism.
* Healthcare fraud was increasing.
* Electronic healthcare systems lacked security controls.

HIPAA introduced:

* Privacy controls
* Security controls
* Breach notification requirements
* Standardized healthcare transactions

---

# Key Terms

### PHI (Protected Health Information)

Any information that identifies a patient and relates to:

* Physical health
* Mental health
* Healthcare services
* Payment for healthcare

Examples:

* Name
* Address
* Medical records
* Lab reports
* Insurance information
* Patient ID

---

### ePHI (Electronic Protected Health Information)

PHI stored electronically.

Examples:

* Electronic Medical Records (EMR)
* Databases
* Cloud storage
* Healthcare applications

---

# 2. HIPAA Regulations

HIPAA regulations define how healthcare organizations must protect PHI and ePHI.

### Organizations Covered by HIPAA

### Covered Entities

1. Healthcare Providers

   * Hospitals
   * Clinics
   * Doctors

2. Health Plans

   * Insurance companies
   * HMOs

3. Healthcare Clearinghouses

   * Medical billing services
   * Data processing organizations

---

### Business Associates

Organizations that handle PHI on behalf of covered entities.

Examples:

* Cloud providers
* Billing companies
* Security vendors
* Data storage providers

Business Associates must sign:

**Business Associate Agreement (BAA)**

---

# 3. HIPAA Titles

HIPAA consists of **5 Titles**.

---

## Title I – Health Insurance Reform

Purpose:

* Protect health insurance coverage.
* Improve portability between jobs.

Key Points:

* Coverage continuity.
* Protection for workers changing jobs.
* Limits pre-existing condition exclusions.

---

## Title II – Administrative Simplification

Most important title for cybersecurity exams.

Purpose:

* Establish security and privacy standards.

Includes:

* Privacy Rule
* Security Rule
* Enforcement Rule
* Transaction Standards

Focus Areas:

* Confidentiality
* Integrity
* Availability

(CIA Triad)

---

## Title III – Tax Related Health Provisions

Purpose:

* Healthcare tax regulations.
* Medical savings accounts.

---

## Title IV – Application and Enforcement of Group Health Plan Requirements

Purpose:

* Additional healthcare coverage requirements.
* Protection against discrimination.

---

## Title V – Revenue Offsets

Purpose:

* Tax deductions related to healthcare.
* Health insurance regulations.

---

# Exam Tip

**Title II = Most Important for Security Professionals**

Remember:

> Privacy + Security + Enforcement

# 4. HIPAA Rules

HIPAA consists of several major rules.

---

# A. Privacy Rule

Implemented: **2003**

Purpose:

Protect patient PHI.

Requirements:

* Limit disclosure of patient data.
* Obtain patient consent when required.
* Provide patient rights.

---

### Patient Rights Under Privacy Rule

Patients can:

✔ Access records

✔ Request corrections

✔ Obtain copies

✔ Know disclosures made

✔ Restrict sharing

---

### Minimum Necessary Standard

Only the minimum amount of information required should be disclosed.

Example:

A receptionist should not view full medical history if only appointment scheduling is needed.

---

# B. Security Rule

Implemented: **2005**

Purpose:

Protect ePHI.

Focus:

* Confidentiality
* Integrity
* Availability

(CIA Triad)

---

## Administrative Safeguards

Policies and procedures.

Examples:

* Risk assessments
* Security training
* Access management
* Incident response plans

---

## Physical Safeguards

Protect physical assets.

Examples:

* Locked server rooms
* CCTV
* Access cards
* Device controls

---

## Technical Safeguards

Technology-based controls.

Examples:

* Encryption
* Authentication
* Audit logs
* Firewalls
* Access control

---

# Security Rule Components

### Confidentiality

Only authorized users can access information.

### Integrity

Data must not be altered improperly.

### Availability

Data should be available when needed.

---

# C. Breach Notification Rule

Implemented: **2009**

Purpose:

Notify affected individuals when PHI is compromised.

---

### Notification Requirements

Notify:

1. Affected individuals
2. Government authorities
3. Media (for large breaches)

---

### Timeline

Must notify affected individuals within:

### 60 Days

of discovering the breach.

---

# D. Enforcement Rule

Purpose:

Defines penalties and investigations.

Regulated by:

### U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS)

---

# Penalties

Violations can result in:

* Fines
* Criminal penalties
* Lawsuits
* Corrective actions

---

# Penalty Categories

| Violation Type              | Example                   |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Unaware                     | Organization did not know |
| Reasonable Cause            | Lack of proper safeguards |
| Willful Neglect Corrected   | Violation fixed           |
| Willful Neglect Uncorrected | Severe violation          |
