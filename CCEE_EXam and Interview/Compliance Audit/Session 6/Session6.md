# 1. SOX REPORTS (Sarbanes-Oxley Act)

## Introduction

The **Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX)** was enacted in **2002** in the United States after major corporate scandals such as:

* Enron
* WorldCom

Purpose:

* Protect investors
* Improve financial reporting accuracy
* Reduce corporate fraud
* Strengthen internal controls

---

## Objectives of SOX

### 1. Financial Transparency

Organizations must provide accurate financial statements.

### 2. Accountability

Senior executives are responsible for correctness of reports.

### 3. Internal Controls

Strong controls must exist to prevent fraud and errors.

### 4. Audit Integrity

Independent auditors verify financial information.

---

## Important SOX Sections

### Section 302

CEO and CFO must certify:

✓ Financial reports are accurate

✓ Internal controls are effective

### Section 404

Most important section for auditors.

Requires:

* Internal Control over Financial Reporting (ICFR)
* Annual assessment of controls
* Independent auditor verification

### Section 409

Requires timely disclosure of material changes affecting finances.

### Section 802

Defines penalties for:

* Destroying records
* Altering evidence
* Fraudulent activities

---

# SOX Reports

A SOX report contains:

## 1. Scope

Systems reviewed:

* ERP
* Payroll
* Financial databases
* Accounting systems

## 2. Risk Assessment

Risks identified:

* Unauthorized access
* Data tampering
* Fraud
* Incorrect financial reporting

## 3. Control Testing

Testing of:

* Access controls
* Change management
* Backup controls
* Audit logs

## 4. Findings

Control weaknesses discovered.

## 5. Recommendations

Corrective actions suggested.

## 6. Management Response

Organization's action plan.

---

# Importance of SOX Reports

Benefits:

✔ Fraud prevention

✔ Better governance

✔ Increased investor confidence

✔ Regulatory compliance

✔ Improved security posture

# 2. SOC REPORTS

## What is SOC?

SOC = **Service Organization Control**

Developed by:

American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA)

Used to evaluate controls of service organizations.

Examples:

* Cloud providers
* Data centers
* SaaS companies
* Managed service providers

---

# Types of SOC Reports

---

## SOC 1

Focus:

Financial reporting controls.

Used when services affect customer financial statements.

Examples:

* Payroll processors
* Financial service providers

### Report Contents

* Control objectives
* Testing performed
* Auditor opinion

---

## SOC 2

Most popular in cybersecurity.

Focus:

Trust Service Criteria (TSC):

1. Security
2. Availability
3. Processing Integrity
4. Confidentiality
5. Privacy

---

### SOC 2 Type I

Evaluates:

Controls at a specific point in time.

Question:

"Are controls designed properly?"

---

### SOC 2 Type II

Evaluates:

Controls over a period of time (usually 6–12 months).

Question:

"Are controls operating effectively?"

---

## SOC 3

Public version of SOC 2.

Contains:

* High-level summary
* No sensitive technical details

Used for:

* Marketing
* Customer trust

---

# Comparison

| Feature             | SOC 1   | SOC 2 | SOC 3 |
| ------------------- | ------- | ----- | ----- |
| Financial Reporting | Yes     | No    | No    |
| Security Controls   | Limited | Yes   | Yes   |
| Detailed Report     | Yes     | Yes   | No    |
| Public Sharing      | No      | No    | Yes   |

# Auditor Process Overview

## Step 1: Planning

Auditor identifies:

* Scope
* Systems
* Risks
* Compliance requirements

---

## Step 2: Understand Controls

Review:

* Policies
* Procedures
* Architecture
* Security controls

---

## Step 3: Risk Assessment

Identify:

* Threats
* Vulnerabilities
* Impact

---

## Step 4: Control Testing

Examples:

### Access Control

Verify:

* MFA
* Password policy
* User reviews

### Logging

Verify:

* Audit logs enabled
* Retention periods

### Change Management

Verify:

* Approval process
* Testing evidence

---

## Step 5: Evidence Collection

Examples:

* Screenshots
* Logs
* Configuration files
* Tickets
* Policies

---

## Step 6: Evaluate Findings

Classify findings:

### Low Risk

Minor issue.

### Medium Risk

Needs improvement.

### High Risk

Immediate action required.

---

## Step 7: Audit Report

Includes:

* Scope
* Methodology
* Findings
* Recommendations
* Auditor opinion

---

# Auditor Opinion Types

## Unqualified Opinion

Best result.

Controls are effective.

---

## Qualified Opinion

Some issues exist.

---

## Adverse Opinion

Major control failures.

---

## Disclaimer Opinion

Insufficient evidence.

# 3. SOX COMPLIANCE AND SECURITY CONTROLS

---

## Access Control

Ensures only authorized users access systems.

Examples:

* RBAC
* MFA
* Least Privilege

Exam Tip:

SOX heavily emphasizes **user access management**.

---

## Authentication Controls

Examples:

* Strong passwords
* MFA
* Account lockout

---

## Authorization Controls

Verify users have proper permissions.

Examples:

* Role-based access
* Segregation of Duties (SoD)

---

## Change Management Controls

Any production change must have:

* Request
* Approval
* Testing
* Documentation

---

## Logging and Monitoring

Collect:

* Login logs
* System logs
* Security logs

Benefits:

* Accountability
* Incident investigation
* Compliance evidence

---

## Backup Controls

Verify:

* Backup frequency
* Backup retention
* Recovery testing

---

## Database Security

Controls:

* Encryption
* Access restrictions
* Monitoring

---

## Network Security

Examples:

* Firewalls
* IDS
* IPS
* Segmentation

---

## Incident Response Controls

Organization should have:

* Incident response plan
* Escalation process
* Investigation procedures

---

## Physical Security Controls

Examples:

* CCTV
* Access cards
* Biometrics
* Visitor logs

---

### 2. Change Management

### 4. Backup and Recovery

# Difference Between SOX and SOC

| SOX                            | SOC                         |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| Law                            | Audit Framework             |
| Mandatory                      | Often Customer Requirement  |
| Focuses on Financial Reporting | Focuses on Service Controls |
| Public Companies               | Service Organizations       |
| Government Regulation          | AICPA Standard              |
