# Session 5: General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

---

# 1. Introduction to GDPR

**GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)** is a data privacy and protection law implemented by the European Union.

### Important Facts

| Item           | Details                                            |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Full Form      | General Data Protection Regulation                 |
| Effective Date | 25 May 2018                                        |
| Region         | European Union (EU) & European Economic Area (EEA) |
| Purpose        | Protect personal data and privacy of individuals   |
| Applies To     | Any organization processing EU residents' data     |
| Penalties      | Up to €20 million or 4% of global annual turnover  |

---

# Why GDPR Was Introduced

Before GDPR:

* Personal data was collected without transparency.
* Users had little control over their information.
* Data breaches were increasing.

GDPR was introduced to:

✔ Protect privacy rights

✔ Increase transparency

✔ Improve data security

✔ Give users control over personal data

---

# 2. Key GDPR Terminology

## Data Subject

Person whose data is being collected.

Example:

* Customer
* Employee
* Student

---

## Personal Data

Any information that can identify a person directly or indirectly.

Examples:

* Name
* Email Address
* Phone Number
* Passport Number
* IP Address
* Location Data
* Employee ID

---

## Data Controller

Organization deciding:

* Why data is collected
* How data is processed

Example:
A company collecting customer information.

---

## Data Processor

Processes data on behalf of controller.

Example:

Cloud providers, payroll companies, hosting services.

---

# 3. Types of Privacy Data Protected Under GDPR

---

## A. Personal Data

Information identifying an individual.

Examples:

* Full Name
* Email
* Mobile Number
* Address
* Date of Birth

---

## B. Sensitive Personal Data (Special Category Data)

Requires stronger protection.

Examples:

### Health Information

* Medical records
* Diseases
* Prescriptions

### Biometric Data

* Fingerprints
* Face Recognition

### Genetic Data

* DNA Information

### Religious Beliefs

* Religion

### Political Opinions

* Political affiliations

### Sexual Orientation

* Sexual preference information

### Ethnic/Racial Origin

* Race
* Ethnicity

---

## C. Online Identifiers

Digital identifiers.

Examples:

* IP Address
* Cookies
* Device IDs
* Browser Fingerprints

---

## D. Financial Data

Examples:

* Credit Card Number
* Bank Account Details
* Payment History

# 4. GDPR Principles

There are **7 Core Principles**.

---

## 1. Lawfulness, Fairness and Transparency

Organizations must:

* Collect data legally
* Inform users clearly

Example:
Privacy policy available to users.

---

## 2. Purpose Limitation

Collect data only for a specific purpose.

Example:

✔ Collect email for account creation

✘ Use it later for unrelated marketing without consent

---

## 3. Data Minimization

Collect only necessary data.

Example:

✔ Name and Email

✘ Asking for passport number unnecessarily

---

## 4. Accuracy

Data must be:

* Correct
* Updated

---

## 5. Storage Limitation

Keep data only as long as required.

Example:

Delete old customer records when no longer needed.

---

## 6. Integrity and Confidentiality

Protect data using:

* Encryption
* Access Control
* Firewalls

---

## 7. Accountability

Organization must prove compliance.

Examples:

* Policies
* Audits
* Documentation

---

# 5. Rights of Data Subjects

GDPR gives users several rights.

---

## Right to be Informed

Users must know:

* What data is collected
* Why it is collected

---

## Right of Access

Users can request their data.

---

## Right to Rectification

Users can correct inaccurate information.

---

## Right to Erasure ("Right to be Forgotten")

Users can request deletion of data.

---

## Right to Restrict Processing

Users can limit how data is used.

---

## Right to Data Portability

Users can transfer their data.

---

## Right to Object

Users can refuse certain processing.

Example:

Marketing emails.

---

## Rights Related to Automated Decision-Making

Protection from decisions made solely by automated systems.

Example:

Loan rejection by AI without human review.

---

# 6. Lawful Bases for Processing Data

Organizations need a valid reason to process data.

### 1. Consent

User agrees.

Example:
Newsletter subscription.

---

### 2. Contract

Necessary to fulfill a contract.

Example:
Online shopping order.

---

### 3. Legal Obligation

Required by law.

Example:
Tax records.

---

### 4. Vital Interests

Protect someone's life.

Example:
Emergency medical treatment.

---

### 5. Public Task

Government activities.

---

### 6. Legitimate Interests

Business interest without violating privacy rights.

Example:
Fraud prevention.

---

# 7. Key Steps to Ensure GDPR Compliance

---

## Step 1: Identify Personal Data

Know:

* What data is collected
* Where it is stored
* Who accesses it

---

## Step 2: Maintain Data Inventory

Create records of:

* Data type
* Purpose
* Storage location

---

## Step 3: Obtain Proper Consent

Consent must be:

✔ Clear

✔ Specific

✔ Freely given

✔ Easy to withdraw

---

## Step 4: Implement Security Controls

Examples:

* Encryption
* MFA
* Access Control
* Antivirus
* Backup

---

## Step 5: Update Privacy Policies

Clearly explain:

* Data collection
* Processing
* Retention

---

## Step 6: Enable Data Subject Rights

Provide mechanisms for:

* Access requests
* Deletion requests
* Corrections

---

## Step 7: Conduct Risk Assessments

Perform:

### DPIA

(Data Protection Impact Assessment)

Required for high-risk processing activities.

---

## Step 8: Manage Third-Party Vendors

Verify vendors follow GDPR.

Examples:

* Cloud Providers
* Payment Processors

---

## Step 9: Report Data Breaches

Organizations must report certain breaches within:

### 72 Hours

to supervisory authorities.

**Exam Point:** 72 Hours is frequently asked.

---

## Step 10: Continuous Monitoring

Regular:

* Audits
* Reviews
* Compliance checks

---

# 5-Mark Answer (Ready for Exam)

**GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)** is a European Union data privacy law that came into effect on **25 May 2018**. It protects personal data and gives individuals greater control over their information. GDPR is based on seven principles: Lawfulness, Purpose Limitation, Data Minimization, Accuracy, Storage Limitation, Integrity & Confidentiality, and Accountability. It provides rights such as access, rectification, erasure, and data portability. Organizations must implement security controls, obtain valid consent, maintain records, and report data breaches within **72 hours**. Non-compliance can result in fines of up to **€20 million or 4% of global annual revenue**.
