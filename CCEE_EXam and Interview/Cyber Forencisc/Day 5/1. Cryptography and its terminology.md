# Cryptography and Its Terminology

## Introduction

Cryptography is the science of securing information and communications through the use of mathematical techniques. It ensures that data remains confidential, maintains its integrity, and is accessible only to authorized users. Cryptography is widely used in cybersecurity, banking, e-commerce, messaging applications, and secure communications.

---

## Objectives of Cryptography

The primary goals of cryptography are:

### 1. Confidentiality

Ensures that information is accessible only to authorized individuals.

**Example:** Encrypting an email so that only the intended recipient can read it.

### 2. Integrity

Ensures that data has not been altered during storage or transmission.

**Example:** Detecting whether a downloaded file has been modified.

### 3. Authentication

Verifies the identity of users, devices, or systems.

**Example:** Confirming that a user is who they claim to be during login.

### 4. Non-Repudiation

Prevents a sender from denying that they sent a message or performed an action.

**Example:** Digital signatures used in legal documents.

---

## Key Cryptographic Terminology

### Plaintext

The original, readable data before encryption.

**Example:** "Hello World"

### Ciphertext

The encrypted form of plaintext that is unreadable without a decryption key.

**Example:** "X7aP9#Lm"

### Encryption

The process of converting plaintext into ciphertext.

### Decryption

The process of converting ciphertext back into plaintext.

### Cipher

An algorithm used to perform encryption and decryption.

**Examples:**

* AES (Advanced Encryption Standard)
* DES (Data Encryption Standard)
* RSA

### Key

A value used by a cryptographic algorithm to encrypt or decrypt data.

**Types of Keys:**

* Symmetric Key
* Public Key
* Private Key

---

## Types of Cryptography

### 1. Symmetric Cryptography

Uses the same key for both encryption and decryption.

#### Advantages

* Fast
* Efficient for large amounts of data

#### Disadvantages

* Secure key distribution is challenging

#### Examples

* AES
* DES
* 3DES

---

### 2. Asymmetric Cryptography

Uses two keys:

* Public Key (shared openly)
* Private Key (kept secret)

#### Advantages

* Secure key exchange
* Supports digital signatures

#### Disadvantages

* Slower than symmetric encryption

#### Examples

* RSA
* ECC (Elliptic Curve Cryptography)

---

### 3. Hash Functions

A hash function converts data into a fixed-length value called a hash or digest.

#### Characteristics

* One-way operation
* Fixed output length
* Sensitive to input changes

#### Examples

* SHA-256
* SHA-512
* MD5 (not recommended for security)

**Use Cases:**

* Password storage
* Data integrity verification
* Digital signatures

---

## Digital Signature

A digital signature is a cryptographic technique used to verify the authenticity and integrity of a message or document.

### Benefits

* Authentication
* Integrity
* Non-repudiation

### Process

1. Generate a hash of the message.
2. Encrypt the hash using the sender's private key.
3. The receiver verifies it using the sender's public key.

---

## Public Key Infrastructure (PKI)

PKI is a framework that manages digital certificates and public-key encryption.

### Components

* Certificate Authority (CA)
* Digital Certificates
* Public and Private Keys

### Purpose

* Secure communication
* Identity verification
* Certificate management

---

## Common Cryptographic Attacks

### Brute Force Attack

Attempting every possible key until the correct one is found.

### Dictionary Attack

Using a predefined list of likely passwords.

### Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) Attack

An attacker intercepts communication between two parties.

### Replay Attack

Capturing and retransmitting valid data transmissions.
