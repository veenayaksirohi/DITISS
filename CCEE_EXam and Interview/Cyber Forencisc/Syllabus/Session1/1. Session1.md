# 1. Introduction to Cyber Forensics

## Definition

Cyber Forensics (Computer Forensics) is the process of identifying, collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence in a legally acceptable manner.

It is a branch of Digital Forensics focused on computers, storage devices, networks, mobile devices, and cloud environments.

### Simple Definition

> Computer Forensics is the science of recovering, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence for investigations and legal proceedings.

---

## Objectives of Computer Forensics

1. Identify digital evidence.
2. Preserve evidence without altering it.
3. Recover deleted or hidden information.
4. Analyze cyber incidents.
5. Determine who performed the activity.
6. Present findings in court or organizational investigations.

---

## Importance of Computer Forensics

* Cybercrime investigation
* Insider threat investigation
* Corporate policy violations
* Data breach investigations
* Incident response
* Legal evidence collection
* Malware analysis

---

## Areas of Digital Forensics

### 1. Computer Forensics

Investigation of desktops, laptops, servers.

### 2. Mobile Forensics

Analysis of smartphones and tablets.

### 3. Network Forensics

Analysis of network traffic and logs.

### 4. Memory Forensics

Analysis of RAM dumps.

### 5. Cloud Forensics

Investigation of cloud-based systems.

### 6. Database Forensics

Investigation of database records and logs.

# Key Terminologies

## Digital Evidence

Any information stored or transmitted in digital form that can be used as evidence.

Examples:

* Emails
* Documents
* Images
* Chat messages
* Browser history
* System logs

---

## Metadata

Data about data.

Examples:

* Creation time
* Modification time
* Owner information
* Access timestamps

---

## Hash Value

A unique fingerprint of a file.

Common Algorithms:

* MD5
* SHA-1
* SHA-256

Purpose:

* Verify integrity of evidence
* Detect modifications

---

## Chain of Custody

Documentation showing:

* Who collected evidence
* When it was collected
* How it was transferred
* Who handled it

Purpose:

* Maintain evidence authenticity

# 2. Difference Between Computer Crime and Unauthorized Activities

## Computer Crime

A criminal act involving computers, networks, or digital devices.

### Examples

* Hacking
* Ransomware attacks
* Data theft
* Identity theft
* Phishing
* Credit card fraud

### Characteristics

* Illegal
* Punishable by law
* Causes financial or operational damage

---

## Unauthorized Activity

Any action performed without permission.

May or may not be criminal.

### Examples

* Accessing another employee's files
* Installing software without approval
* Using office systems for personal purposes
* Viewing confidential documents

---

## Comparison Table

| Computer Crime                  | Unauthorized Activity             |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Illegal by law                  | Violation of policy or permission |
| Criminal offense                | May not be criminal               |
| Punishable by court             | Usually disciplinary action       |
| Causes direct harm              | May not cause harm                |
| Investigated by law enforcement | Investigated internally           |

---

## Example

### Unauthorized Activity

Employee accesses manager's files without permission.

### Computer Crime

Employee steals and sells confidential data.

# 3. Cyber Laws

## Definition

Cyber laws regulate activities involving computers, networks, and digital information.

They provide legal recognition and punishment for cyber offenses.

---

# Information Technology Act, 2000 (India)

The primary cyber law in India.

### Objectives

* Legal recognition of electronic records
* Legal recognition of digital signatures
* Prevention of cyber crimes
* E-commerce regulation

---

## Important Sections

### Section 43

Unauthorized access and damage to computer systems.

Penalty:
Compensation for damages.

---

### Section 65

Tampering with computer source documents.

Punishment:
Up to 3 years imprisonment or fine.

---

### Section 66

Computer-related offenses.

Examples:

* Hacking
* Data alteration

Punishment:
Up to 3 years imprisonment and/or fine.

---

### Section 66C

Identity Theft

Examples:

* Password theft
* Digital signature misuse

Punishment:
Up to 3 years imprisonment and fine.

---

### Section 66D

Cheating by Personation

Examples:

* Online fraud
* Fake profiles

Punishment:
Up to 3 years imprisonment and fine.

---

### Section 66E

Violation of Privacy

Examples:

* Capturing private images without consent

Punishment:
Up to 3 years imprisonment.

---

### Section 67

Publishing Obscene Content Online

Punishment:
Imprisonment and fine.

# 4. Process of Computer Forensics

---

## Phase 1: Identification

Identify potential evidence sources.

Examples:

* Hard disks
* USB drives
* RAM
* Cloud accounts
* Mobile phones

---

## Phase 2: Preservation

Protect evidence from modification.

Activities:

* Disconnect network
* Use write blockers
* Create forensic image

Important Rule:

> Never work on original evidence.

---

## Phase 3: Collection

Acquire evidence.

Methods:

* Disk imaging
* Memory acquisition
* Log collection

Tools:

* FTK Imager
* dd
* Guymager
* Magnet Acquire

---

## Phase 4: Examination

Extract useful information.

Examples:

* Recover deleted files
* Extract browser history
* Analyze registry

---

## Phase 5: Analysis

Determine:

* What happened?
* When did it happen?
* Who did it?
* How was it done?

Tools:

* Volatility
* Autopsy
* EnCase
* FTK

---

## Phase 6: Documentation

Record every action.

Include:

* Date
* Time
* Tools used
* Findings

---

## Phase 7: Presentation

Prepare report.

Present findings to:

* Management
* Investigators
* Court

---

# Forensic Investigation Flow

```text
Incident
    ↓
Identification
    ↓
Preservation
    ↓
Collection
    ↓
Examination
    ↓
Analysis
    ↓
Documentation
    ↓
Presentation
```

# Live vs Dead Forensics

## Live Forensics

System is running.

Examples:

* RAM acquisition
* Active processes
* Network connections

Advantages:

* Capture volatile data

Disadvantages:

* System changes continuously

---

## Dead Forensics

System is powered off.

Examples:

* Disk analysis
* File system analysis

Advantages:

* Less risk of evidence alteration

Disadvantages:

* Volatile data lost

---

# Volatile vs Non-Volatile Data

## Volatile Data

Lost after power off.

Examples:

* RAM
* Running processes
* Network sessions

---

## Non-Volatile Data

Remains after shutdown.

Examples:

* Hard disk data
* SSD data
* USB data

---

# Order of Volatility (Important Exam Question)

Collect in this order:

```text
CPU Cache
↓
RAM
↓
Running Processes
↓
Network Connections
↓
Disk Data
↓
Archives/Backups
```
