# 1. Forensics Implications

## Introduction

Cyber forensics involves collecting, preserving, analyzing, and presenting digital evidence in a manner acceptable in court.

The implications of digital forensics affect:

* Legal proceedings
* Organizational security
* Privacy rights
* Evidence integrity
* Incident response

---

## Importance of Digital Evidence

Digital evidence may be found in:

* Computers
* Mobile devices
* Servers
* Cloud systems
* Network logs
* USB devices
* IoT devices

Examples:

* Emails
* Chat records
* Browser history
* Log files
* Deleted files
* Metadata

---

## Legal Implications

Investigators must ensure:

### Admissibility

Evidence must be legally collected.

### Authenticity

Evidence must be genuine and untampered.

### Reliability

Methods used must be scientifically accepted.

### Integrity

Evidence must remain unchanged.

---

## Consequences of Improper Handling

If evidence is mishandled:

* Evidence may become inadmissible.
* Investigation may fail.
* Organization may face legal action.
* Wrong person may be accused.

---

## Chain of Custody

A documented history showing:

* Who collected evidence
* When collected
* Who handled it
* Where it was stored

Purpose:
Ensures integrity and accountability.

---

## Challenges in Cyber Forensics

### Encryption

Encrypted files are difficult to analyze.

### Anti-Forensics

Techniques used to hide evidence.

Examples:

* File wiping
* Steganography
* Log deletion

### Cloud Computing

Data may reside in multiple countries.

### Large Data Volumes

Big data increases analysis complexity.

---

# Exam Point

**Digital evidence must be:**

1. Authentic
2. Reliable
3. Complete
4. Believable

# 2. Accreditation Standards

## Definition

Accreditation is formal recognition that a forensic laboratory meets specific standards and is competent to perform forensic investigations.

---

## Objectives

* Maintain quality
* Ensure consistency
* Improve credibility
* Increase court acceptance

---

## Benefits

### Quality Assurance

Ensures accurate results.

### Standard Procedures

All investigators follow same methodology.

### Legal Acceptance

Courts trust accredited labs.

### Continuous Improvement

Regular audits improve performance.

---

## Major Accreditation Standards

### ISO/IEC 17025

Most important standard for forensic laboratories.

Focuses on:

* Competence
* Impartiality
* Consistency

---

### ISO 9001

Quality Management System.

Focuses on:

* Customer satisfaction
* Process improvement

---

### NIST Guidelines

Published by:

National Institute of Standards and Technology

Provides:

* Digital forensic procedures
* Testing methodologies
* Security standards

---

### SWGDE

Scientific Working Group on Digital Evidence

Provides:

* Best practices
* Investigation guidelines
* Evidence handling standards

---

## Accreditation Requirements

### Personnel Competency

Investigators must be trained.

### Equipment Validation

Tools must be tested.

### Documentation

Every activity must be recorded.

### Quality Control

Regular audits are required.

---

# ISO 17025 Key Components

### Management Requirements

* Documentation
* Audits
* Corrective actions

### Technical Requirements

* Staff competency
* Equipment calibration
* Method validation

---

# Exam Point

**Most important forensic laboratory accreditation standard:**
➡ ISO/IEC 17025

# 3. Performing a Cyber Forensics Investigation

## Investigation Lifecycle

### Step 1: Preparation

Prepare:

* Tools
* Team
* Procedures

Examples:

* FTK Imager
* Autopsy
* EnCase
* Volatility

---

### Step 2: Identification

Identify:

* Systems involved
* Potential evidence sources

Examples:

* Hard disks
* RAM
* Network logs

---

### Step 3: Preservation

Protect evidence from alteration.

Actions:

* Disconnect network
* Use write blockers
* Create forensic images

---

### Step 4: Collection

Acquire evidence.

Methods:

### Live Acquisition

System remains powered on.

Collect:

* RAM
* Active processes
* Network connections

---

### Dead Acquisition

System is powered off.

Collect:

* Disk image
* Storage devices

---

### Step 5: Examination

Extract relevant information.

Examples:

* Deleted files
* Registry entries
* Event logs

---

### Step 6: Analysis

Correlate findings.

Questions answered:

* What happened?
* When did it happen?
* Who performed it?
* How was it done?

---

### Step 7: Reporting

Create detailed report.

Include:

* Scope
* Methodology
* Findings
* Evidence list
* Conclusion

---

### Step 8: Presentation

Present findings to:

* Management
* Law enforcement
* Court

---

# Order of Volatility

Collect evidence in this order:

1. CPU Registers
2. Cache
3. RAM
4. Network Connections
5. Running Processes
6. Disk
7. Backup Media

### Rule

**Most volatile data first.**

---

## Investigation Principles

### Principle 1

Do not alter evidence.

### Principle 2

Document every action.

### Principle 3

Maintain chain of custody.

### Principle 4

Use validated tools.

# 4. Privacy and Cyber Forensics

## Definition

Privacy refers to an individual's right to control personal information.

Cyber forensic investigations must balance:

* Evidence collection
* Individual privacy rights

---

## Privacy Concerns

### Personal Information

Examples:

* Emails
* Photos
* Medical records
* Financial records

---

### Employee Monitoring

Organizations may investigate employee systems.

Issues:

* Consent
* Monitoring policies
* Data ownership

---

### Cloud Data

Challenges:

* Multiple jurisdictions
* Third-party ownership
* Shared infrastructure

---

## Legal Requirements

Investigators must:

### Obtain Authorization

Examples:

* Search warrant
* Court order
* Organizational approval

---

### Follow Regulations

Examples:

* GDPR
* Data Protection Laws
* Privacy Acts

---

## Privacy Principles

### Data Minimization

Collect only required evidence.

### Purpose Limitation

Use data only for investigation.

### Confidentiality

Protect collected information.

### Accountability

Maintain proper documentation.

---

## Ethical Considerations

Investigators should:

* Respect privacy
* Avoid unnecessary access
* Protect sensitive information
* Maintain professionalism

---

## Challenges

### BYOD (Bring Your Own Device)

Personal and corporate data coexist.

### Cloud Storage

Ownership issues arise.

### Encrypted Devices

Access may require legal approval.

---

# Best Practices

✔ Obtain authorization first

✔ Collect only relevant data

✔ Maintain confidentiality

✔ Secure evidence storage

✔ Follow legal procedures

✔ Document everything
