1️⃣ Twisted-Pair Cable

Definition

  • A twisted pair consists of two conductors (normally copper), each with its own plastic insulation, twisted together.
  • One of the wires is used to carry signals to the receiver.
  • The other wire is used only as a ground reference.
  • The receiver uses the difference between the two.
  • In addition to the signal from the sender, interference (noise) and crosstalk may affect both wires and create unwanted signals.
  • If the two wires are parallel, the unwanted signals are not equal because they are at different locations relative to noise sources.
  • This results in a difference at the receiver.
  • By twisting the wires, a balance is maintained.

UTP and STP

  • The most common twisted-pair cable is UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair).
  • IBM produced another type called STP (Shielded Twisted Pair).
  • STP cable has metal foil or braided-mesh covering around each pair.
  • This prevents noise and crosstalk.
  • However, STP is bulkier and more expensive.
  • Twisted-pair cables are used in telephone lines.
  • The local loop connects subscribers to telephone office.

2️⃣ Coaxial Cable

Definition

  • Coaxial cable carries higher frequency signals than twisted pair.
  • It has a central core conductor.
  • The core is enclosed in insulating sheath.
  • Then covered by metal foil or braid.
  • The outer metal works as:
    • Shield against noise
    • Second conductor
  • The cable is protected by plastic cover.

Usage

  • Used in analog telephone networks.
  • One coax cable carried 10,000 voice signals.
  • Digital telephone networks supported 600 Mbps.
  • Widely used in Cable TV networks.
  • Used in Early Ethernet LANs.
  • Now replaced by fiber optic.

3️⃣ Fiber-Optic Cable

Definition

  • Made of glass or plastic.
  • Transmits signals as light.
  • Uses reflection to guide light.
  • The core is surrounded by cladding.
  • The density difference ensures light reflection.

Usage

  • Used in backbone networks.
  • Used in hybrid networks.
  • Used in 100Base-FX.
  • Used in 1000Base-X.
  • Used in 10-Gigabit Ethernet.

⚠ Crosstalk Definition

Crosstalk refers to interference or unwanted signals in a communication system. It occurs when signals from one cable interfere with another. Effects:
  • Signal distortion
  • Noise increase
  • Reduced communication quality

📻 Radio Waves

  • Radio waves are electromagnetic waves.
  • They travel in all directions.
  • Do not require line-of-sight.
  • Can penetrate walls.
  • Used in:
    • Radio Broadcasting
    • Television
    • Wireless Communication

📡 Microwaves

  • Microwaves travel in straight lines.
  • Require line-of-sight communication.
  • Use directional antennas.
  • Cannot penetrate obstacles easily.
  • Used in:
    • Satellite Communication
    • Mobile Networks
    • Radar Systems

🔴 Infrared Waves

  • Infrared waves have short range.
  • Cannot pass through walls.
  • Used for very short-distance communication.
  • Used in:
    • TV Remote Controls
    • Short-range Devices

📌 LAN Cable Categories Explanation

  • Cat5 — Used in older Ethernet networks.
  • Cat5e — Improved Cat5 with reduced crosstalk.
  • Cat6 — Supports faster speeds and better performance.
  • Cat6a — Enhanced Cat6 for long-distance high speed.
  • Cat7 — Shielded cable for high-speed networks.
  • Cat8 — Used in data centers with very high speed.
